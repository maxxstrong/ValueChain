The deals and
the policies

Trade data: UN Comtrade, calendar year 2025 (series 2016–2025). State data: DGCIS / NIRYAT, Q2 FY2025-26 (July–September 2025) and FY 2024-25 (April 2024 – March 2025). Page built 26 July 2026.

Two things that decide where Indian goods can go: the trade agreements that open borders, and what governments — central and state — do to push goods through them. Both, tied back to the live numbers on this site.

01

India’s trade agreements, sector by sector

Four agreements now shape market access for Indian exporters. What each one actually changes:

India–UAE CEPA

In force since May 2022

The UAE is India’s second-largest export market: $38.7 bn in 2025 — and its biggest re-export hub into Africa and the Gulf.

  • Gems & jewellery — zero duty on plain gold jewellery within an annual quota; the UAE is already the top buyer of Indian jewellery (and India’s top gems supplier in turn).
  • Textiles, leather, footwear — immediate duty elimination, aimed at re-export through Dubai’s trading houses.
  • Pharma — fast-track regulatory approval for Indian medicines already approved in developed markets.
  • About 97% of UAE tariff lines opened to Indian goods, covering 99% of exports by value.

India–Australia ECTA

In force since Dec 2022 · full zero-duty since Jan 2026

India exported $7.7 bn to Australia in 2025. The jump came before the agreement — $3.5 bn in 2020 to $8.2 bn in 2022 — and exports have been broadly flat since ECTA entered force at the end of that year. From 1 January 2026, 100% of Indian exports enter Australia duty-free, so the test of the deal is still ahead.

  • Textiles & apparel, leather, gems, sports goods — the labour-intensive sectors got zero duty from day one.
  • Pharma — fast-track approval route mirroring the UK/Canada assessments.
  • In return India cut duties on Australian coal, wool, lentils and wine (wine phased down, not eliminated).
  • A deeper agreement (CECA) covering services, digital trade and critical minerals is still under negotiation.

India–EFTA TEPA (Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein)

In force since Oct 2025

The unusual one: EFTA states committed to invest $100 bn in India over 15 years — the first FTA anywhere to bind investment to market access. Switzerland is India’s 11th-largest import source (mostly gold — see who supplies India’s gems).

  • India phases out tariffs on ~95% of industrial imports from EFTA over up to 10 years — watches, chocolate, machinery, pharma inputs get cheaper.
  • Gold — by far the biggest EFTA export to India — keeps its duty structure largely intact.
  • Indian exporters gain duty-free access for textiles, chemicals and engineering goods, plus easier services mobility.

India–UK CETA

In force since 15 July 2026 — this month

Signed July 2025, live as of this month. The UK is India’s fifth-largest export market: $13.8 bn in 2025; both sides expect bilateral trade to roughly double by 2030.

  • The UK scrapped duties on 99% of Indian tariff lines immediately — ending 12% tariffs on textiles & clothing, 16% on leather & footwear, 21.5% on marine products, 18% on engineering goods, up to 70% on processed foods.
  • Biggest expected winners: apparel (HS 62, 61), footwear (64), gems, auto components — sectors where India competes head-on with Bangladesh and Vietnam (see the race).
  • India phases Scotch whisky duties from 150% to 75% now, 40% by year ten; UK cars enter under a quota with falling rates.
  • A social-security pact (no double contributions for posted workers) came into force the same day.

What is your state doing about it?

Pick a state to see the reforms, policies and subsidies its government runs to push exports — plus the central schemes that apply everywhere.

How this list is built. Policy details are curated by hand for the sixteen biggest exporter states, from official state policy documents (e.g. UP Export Promotion Policy 2025–30, Maharashtra Export Policy 2023) — last checked July 2026. Smaller states and UTs show the national schemes that apply to them. Policies change; treat this as an orientation, not legal advice, and verify current terms on the state’s own portal before applying.
