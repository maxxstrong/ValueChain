About this data

Source: UN Comtrade, annual imports reported by the United States (reporter 842) and the European Union (reporter 97, extra-EU trade), valued CIF in current US dollars. All 40 product groups tracked by this site are available on both charts — the four buttons are shortcuts that bundle chapters together, and Custom opens the full list. The bundles are: textiles & apparel = HS 50–63; pharmaceuticals = 30; leather & footwear = 41–43 and 64; electronics = 85. EU customs-procedure sub-records are aggregated to totals. Every chart’s underlying numbers can be downloaded as CSV.

Two definitions worth knowing before you quote these numbers. Electronics here is HS 85 — electrical and electronic equipment. That covers phones, which is the story of the last five years, but it excludes computers and laptops: those sit in HS 8471, inside the machinery chapter (HS 84). So this is a comparison of electrical goods and phones, not of all electronics. Vietnam’s laptop assembly in particular is understated by this definition. Leather & footwear is HS 41–43 plus 64, which includes HS 43 (furskins and artificial fur) — a small chapter most readers would not think of as leather. It stays in for continuity with the standard grouping, and it moves the totals very little.
