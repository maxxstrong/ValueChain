Data story No. 7 · World Bank

The half of India’s trade
most charts leave out

Trade data: UN Comtrade, calendar year 2025 (series 2016–2025). State data: DGCIS / NIRYAT, Q2 FY2025-26 (July–September 2025) and FY 2024-25 (April 2024 – March 2025). Page built 26 July 2026.

Every other page on this site counts goods. But India sells almost as much in services — software, business services, travel — and unlike goods, that trade runs a large surplus. Leave it out and you get India’s trade position badly wrong.

What India actually sells

Composition of India’s services exports.

The World Bank groups computer services, communications and other business services together, so this single share covers software exports, IT-enabled services and the global capability centres that multinationals run from India. It is the closest thing India has to a dominant export product — and it faces no tariffs, no ports and no shipping.

About this data

Source: World Bank balance-of-payments statistics, current US dollars, refreshed automatically alongside the rest of the site. Services trade is not in UN Comtrade, which is why it needs a different source — and why it is missing from most trade dashboards.

Two caveats worth stating. Services data lags goods data by about a year, so the latest year here is earlier than elsewhere on the site. And balance-of-payments services figures are not directly comparable with customs-based goods figures: they are compiled differently, from different returns. The goods line on this page comes from India’s own UN Comtrade reporting and is shown for scale, not as an exact like-for-like.
