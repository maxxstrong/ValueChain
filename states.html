Data story No. 6 · DGCIS, Ministry of Commerce

Who inside India
does the exporting?

Trade data: UN Comtrade, calendar year 2025 (series 2016–2025). State data: DGCIS / NIRYAT, Q2 FY2025-26 (July–September 2025) and FY 2024-25 (April 2024 – March 2025). Page built 27 July 2026.

India’s exports are startlingly concentrated: a handful of states — and even a handful of districts — carry the whole country. Here is the map, from Gujarat’s refineries to Noida’s phone factories.

The full-year league table

Merchandise exports, FY 2024-25 (April 2024 – March 2025) — the eight biggest exporter states.

Gujarat alone is about a quarter of India’s exports — more than Maharashtra and Karnataka combined. Mineral fuels are roughly a third of what the state ships, most of that leaving through the Jamnagar refinery complex.

02

Every state and union territory

All 36, ranked — FY 2022-23 (April 2022 – March 2023), the most recent year for which the Ministry publishes complete coverage of every state and UT.

The long tail is stark: the eight North-Eastern states together export less than Goa alone. Landlocked states without mineral or industrial bases barely register — the geography of ports is the geography of trade.

03

The ten districts that carry India

Top exporting districts, Q2 FY2025-26 (July–September 2025) — together nearly 37% of everything India ships.

Four of the ten are in Gujarat, three in Maharashtra. Noida (Gautam Buddha Nagar) is Uttar Pradesh’s entry — see what each state government does for its exporters on the explainers page. This concentration is structural: it barely moves from quarter to quarter.

What this card can and cannot show. Official data thins out below the biggest states, so this card shows each tier honestly rather than guessing: the top 5 states get their full five-commodity breakdown with percentage shares (DGCIS publishes this only for them); the top 15 states also get their Q2 FY2025-26 export value (DGCIS reports quarterly figures only that far down); every other state and UT shows its full-year export value and officially recorded leading export from FY 2022-23 — the most recent year with complete coverage of all 36. All figures are merchandise (goods) only, from the Ministry of Commerce (DGCIS/NIRYAT) — not UN Comtrade, which has no state-level data. Nothing on this card is estimated or extrapolated.

05

The quarterly snapshot

Merchandise exports by state, Q2 FY2025-26 (July–September 2025) — a single quarter, not an annual rate.

Read this one with care. A quarter is a short window: monsoon shipping, festival timing and a single large consignment can all move a state several places. The annual table above is the reliable ranking; this card shows the most recent official quarter for currency, and is superseded whenever DGCIS publishes the next review. Year-on-year growth figures per state are in the CSV rather than on the chart, because quarter-on-quarter percentages are the most volatile numbers on this site.

About this data

Unlike the rest of this site, state-level trade does not come from UN Comtrade (which only has national data). This page uses the DGCIS Quarterly Review of Merchandise Foreign Trade (Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Government of India) — quarterly and district figures from the July–September 2025 review (released November 2025) — and full-year state figures from the Ministry’s NIRYAT portal. Merchandise (goods) only; services excluded throughout. These figures are updated by hand when DGCIS publishes a new quarterly review — they are not refreshed by scripts/fetch_data.py.
