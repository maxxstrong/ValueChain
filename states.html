Who inside India
does the exporting?

India’s exports are startlingly concentrated: a handful of states — and even a handful of districts — carry the whole country. Here is the map, from Gujarat’s refineries to Noida’s phone factories.

What this card can and cannot show. Official data thins out below the biggest states, so this card shows each tier honestly rather than guessing: the top 5 states get their full five-commodity breakdown with percentage shares (DGCIS publishes this only for them); the top 15 states get their latest-quarter export value and year-on-year growth (DGCIS reports quarterly figures only that far down); every other state and UT shows its full-year export value and officially recorded leading export from FY 2022-23 — the most recent year with complete coverage of all 36. All figures are merchandise (goods) only, from the Ministry of Commerce (DGCIS/NIRYAT) — not UN Comtrade, which has no state-level data. Nothing on this card is estimated or extrapolated.

The long tail is stark: the entire North-East together exports less than a single mid-sized Gujarat district. Landlocked states without mineral or industrial bases barely register — the geography of ports is the geography of trade.

About this data

Unlike the rest of this site, state-level trade does not come from UN Comtrade (which only has national data). This page uses the DGCIS Quarterly Review of Merchandise Foreign Trade (Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Government of India) — quarterly and district figures from the July–September 2025 review (released November 2025) — and full-year state figures from the Ministry’s NIRYAT portal. Merchandise (goods) only; services excluded throughout. These figures are updated by hand when DGCIS publishes a new quarterly review — they are not refreshed by scripts/fetch_data.py.
